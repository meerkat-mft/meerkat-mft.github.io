What is it?

File exchange for businesses that can't lose track.

Companies send files to banks, suppliers, and partners all day. Payroll, invoices, patient records, orders.

Managed file transfer makes that automatic, encrypted, and provable. Meerkat is a new take on it, designed so the system in charge never holds the keys to your files.

Why it exists

File transfer servers keep making headlines.

Internet-facing file transfer software has become a favourite target, because one flaw can expose everything passing through it. Meerkat starts from a different question: what if the system in charge added no internet-facing server, and could not read the files it directs?

The idea

It sees every delivery. Not what's inside.

Meerkat is a managed file transfer orchestrator that will be open source. Agents on your own systems do the reading and writing. The orchestrator is designed to direct and record every delivery while file contents and credentials stay unreadable to it.

Standing orders

Three rules the design doesn't bend.

  1. 01

    Nothing needs to face the internet.

    Agents dial out, and the orchestrator and relay belong on a private network. You bring your own partner endpoints, so Meerkat adds no internet-facing server to attack.

  2. 02

    The orchestrator can't read your files or your credentials.

    Files are encrypted between agents, and endpoint credentials are sealed to the agents that use them.

  3. 03

    No file moves without evidence.

    Audit records are hash-chained, signed, and checkable outside the database.

The route

Two agents. One blind relay.

Neither agent accepts a connection. Both reach out to the relay, which forwards ciphertext it cannot open.

Visual flows

Your infrastructure

Runs where your data lives.

Meerkat is designed to be self-hosted, on any cloud or in your own data centre. There is no vendor service in the middle, so your files stay in your environment and the region you choose.

Compliance

Evidence, not promises.

Audit trails, access control, encryption, and retention settings are designed to support your GDPR, NIS2, and DORA work. Those laws bind organizations, not software, so Meerkat is designed to help you show compliance rather than claim it.

What's planned

On the drawing board.

  1. Connects to what you have

    Local files first, then SFTP, S3, Azure Blob, and SMB.

  2. API first

    Everything in the console is available through a REST API, for CLIs and ITSM tools.

  3. Built for failure

    Interrupted deliveries retry and resume, and source files stay until every delivery succeeds.

  4. Careful engineering

    Services written in Go, with signed releases, SBOMs, and no custom cryptography.

Planned. None of this is built yet.
